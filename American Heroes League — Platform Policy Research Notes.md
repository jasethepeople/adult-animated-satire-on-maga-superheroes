# American Heroes League — Platform Policy Research Notes

## YouTube

YouTube requires creators to disclose AI-generated or meaningfully altered content when it makes a real person appear to say or do something they did not do, alters real events or places, or generates realistic scenes that did not occur. The upload flow provides an AI-use setting, and YouTube may add labels. YouTube also prohibits misleading impersonation, including AI likeness or voice used to suggest that an individual owns, authorizes, or endorses content. A clear disclosure does not override the impersonation policy. [1] [2]

**Strategy implication:** Use clearly non-photorealistic cartoon art, original voices, a distinct series channel name/handle, and a written parody notice. Review the AI-use field on every upload and disclose if the particular content meets YouTube’s criteria or could appear realistic.

## X

X permits compliant Parody, Commentary, and Fan accounts when the purpose is to discuss, satirize, or share information, but prohibits deceptive impersonation and manipulated media that may cause widespread confusion on public issues or serious harm. [3]

**Strategy implication:** Create an original brand account rather than an account styled as a public figure. Use transparent parody labeling in account name/bio and do not present clips as real statements, endorsements, or news footage.

## Rumble

Rumble’s support materials state that its community guidelines prohibit copyrighted material, hate speech, and sexually explicit content. Its 2026 Creator Program says eligible original content must show the creator or an avatar persona; its program requirements can change, and no payment is guaranteed. [4] [5] [6]

**Strategy implication:** Treat Rumble as the long-form premiere and community platform. Use original or licensed assets only, operate an identifiable series/creator avatar, and view creator-program monetization as an optional later-stage path rather than launch revenue. Confirm the current program requirements before applying.

## BitChute

BitChute’s guidelines hold creators responsible for content and metadata, require appropriate content-sensitivity settings, prohibit misleading metadata and impersonation intended to trick viewers, and expressly recognize satire, parody, humor/comedy, and works of fiction as contextual exemptions in its content rules. Its terms state that creators are responsible for content rights, including third-party IP, publicity, and privacy rights. [7] [8] [9]

**Strategy implication:** Treat BitChute as a secondary archive and community channel. Use a clearly fictional title/description, avoid misleading thumbnails and metadata, select the correct sensitivity setting, and retain a rights log for animation, music, voices, graphics, and uploaded masters.

## Verified Audience / Distribution Context

Pew reported in 2025 that 53% of U.S. adults get news from social media at least sometimes; 35% reported regularly getting news on YouTube and 12% on X. Rumble reported average global monthly active users of 52 million in Q4 2025; this is company-reported rather than independently audited audience research. [10] [11]

**Strategy implication:** Use YouTube for broad video discovery, X for high-intent discussion and clip circulation, and Rumble for long-form community release. Do not frame these data as a guaranteed audience or revenue outcome.
