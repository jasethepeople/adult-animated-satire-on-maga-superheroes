# Pandora AI Proof-of-Concept Scope

## Objective

Produce a **75–90 second animated proof of concept** for *American Heroes League*. The test should verify whether Pandora AI can sustain a single satirical 2D-cartoon look, keep two recurring characters visually recognizable, create usable short motion clips, and generate material that can be edited into a coherent introduction.

The proof of concept is intentionally not a full episode. A 20–22 minute episode should be divided into many short, controllable shots; attempting to generate it as one prompt would make character drift, pacing, dialogue synchronization, and continuity much harder to manage.

> **Creative and production guardrail:** This is a fictional parody in an alternate superhero universe. Treat named public figures as highly stylized caricatures, avoid presenting invented events or allegations as factual, and make the parody notice prominent in the episode description and end card.

## Recommended Test Sequence: “The Warning”

The sequence adapts the Mar-a-Lago/JFK-ghost moment from the pilot. It is compact enough to test the workflow while showcasing the intended dark, dry tone and the project’s visual identity.

| Element | Production choice |
| --- | --- |
| Working title | **American Heroes League: The Warning** |
| Runtime | 75–90 seconds |
| Format | 16:9, 1920×1080, 24 fps if available |
| Animation approach | 2D adult-cartoon satire; limited but deliberate motion rather than photorealism |
| Core cast | A fictionalized, exaggerated Commander-in-Chief parody character and a fictionalized spectral presidential-mentor figure |
| Location | Lavish fictional Florida estate office during a stormy evening |
| Dialogue approach | Record voiceover separately, then edit visuals to the final audio; do not rely on native generated lip-sync for the first test |
| Music | Low-key patriotic parody cue shifting to eerie office ambience; use only music licensed for commercial distribution |
| Output | 8–10 short video shots, each 4–10 seconds, plus clean still character references |

## Success Criteria

The test is successful if it can be assembled into a short clip in which the character silhouettes, costumes, colors, and overall art direction remain clear. It does **not** need perfect facial continuity or sophisticated dialogue lip-sync on the first pass.

| Criterion | Pass condition | What to do if it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Character continuity | The Commander-in-Chief and spectral mentor remain recognizable from shot to shot | Use approved reference images and repeat the character anchor block verbatim in every prompt |
| Style consistency | Shots read as part of one flat 2D satirical cartoon | Add the global style lock and avoid switching terms such as “cinematic realism” or “3D render” |
| Motion clarity | Each shot contains one understandable action | Shorten the action to one motion; split multi-action beats into separate shots |
| Editorial continuity | Transitions can be cut together without obvious confusion | Use matching screen direction and begin/end poses in adjacent shots |
| Dialogue usability | Voiceover can be matched convincingly enough with cutaways and reaction shots | Reserve close-ups for short lines and use over-the-shoulder shots during longer dialogue |
| Brand safety | No generated factual-looking news claims, fabricated documents, or real-world endorsements | Use clearly fictional in-world newspaper graphics with no legible factual headlines; add a parody notice |

## Proof-of-Concept Script

The dialogue is deliberately short so visual editing can hide imperfect mouth movement.

> **NARRATOR:** In an alternate America, the paperwork had become… sentient.
>
> **COMMANDER-IN-CHIEF:** That is an alarming amount of paperwork. Frankly, it’s very organized. Suspiciously organized.
>
> **SPECTRAL MENTOR:** The machinery of control is waking, Commander. You cannot confront it alone.
>
> **COMMANDER-IN-CHIEF:** Alone? I have an office, a cape-tie, and a very dramatic weather system.
>
> **SPECTRAL MENTOR:** You will need a league.
>
> **COMMANDER-IN-CHIEF:** Fine. But I’m naming it.
>
> **NARRATOR:** The American Heroes League. Coming soon… assuming the paperwork clears.

## Editorial Workflow

First, write and record the final voiceover. Second, generate the two character-reference sheets and approve them. Third, make the ten shots one at a time, using the same visual anchor language and the approved reference images. Fourth, assemble the clips in DaVinci Resolve, CapCut, Adobe Premiere Pro, or another editor. Add sound effects and music only after the picture lock.

Do not post unedited raw generations. They are production ingredients, not final episodes. Label the final upload with a clear fictional-parody notice and ensure any references to real people are not represented as factual reporting or endorsement.

## Decision Gate After the Test

After exporting the proof of concept, review whether Pandora AI can maintain the characters with the desired quality. If the results are strong, build the pilot in **90–150 short shots** grouped into sequences. If the results are inconsistent, use Pandora AI primarily for backgrounds, transitions, and cutaways, then combine it with a character-animation workflow or human animation cleanup.
