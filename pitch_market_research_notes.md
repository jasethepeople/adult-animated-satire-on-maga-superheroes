# Pitch Deck Market Research Notes

## Verified Findings

| Finding | Pitch implication | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pew’s August 2025 survey found that 53% of U.S. adults get news from social media at least sometimes. | A short-form, video-led discovery strategy is aligned with an established social-news behavior. | [1] |
| In the same Pew fact sheet, 35% of U.S. adults reported regularly getting news on YouTube, while 12% said the same for X. | YouTube should be framed as the broad-reach discovery layer; X is valuable as a high-intent discussion and amplification layer rather than the sole audience engine. | [1] |
| Pew’s 2024 survey found that 21% of U.S. adults regularly get news from social-media news influencers, rising to 37% among adults ages 18–29. The study also found 59% of sampled news influencers monetized their online presence in some way. | Supports a creator-led model built around clips, personalities, community, memberships, and merchandise—without claiming that the series will necessarily reach a specific audience. | [2] |
| Rumble reported average global MAUs of 52 million in Q4 2025 and said Rumble Shorts had surpassed 1 million daily unique video views in early 2026. | Rumble can be positioned as a meaningful secondary premiere/community channel, especially for short teaser cuts and independent-media audiences. These are company-reported measures, not an independent estimate. | [3] |

## Pitch Boundaries

1. Do not claim a guaranteed market size, viewer count, ROI, platform approval, or distribution deal.
2. Present the series as a differentiated, clearly fictional adult-animation IP rather than as news, documentary, or political campaign content.
3. Describe the dual-path distribution plan as an option: broad-reach discovery via YouTube and social clips; community-led long-form launch via independent video platforms.
4. Place all generated financial figures in the deck as **illustrative planning assumptions to be validated**, not historical facts.
